# Architecture

<ArchitectureOverview />

## Identity and registration

An organization may know whom it serves and retain the evidence needed for its own checks. The network needs a way to recognize that participant without placing their personal identity on Ethereum.

For someone using their own wallet, the network identity comes from cryptographic keys generated on their device. The wallet derives a **network ID** from those keys, not from a name, document number, or biometric. Ethereum records that ID and public receiving information. The private keys stay on the person's device.

Registration checks recognize reuse of the same wallet identity, not every possible identity belonging to one person.

Holding your own keys is the **self sovereign** part of this flow. Keeping personal records off chain also does not make the participant anonymous to the organization that enrolled them.

## Credentials

The organization can then issue a **credential**, a signed record of approval, to the wallet.

<CredentialFlow />

A **zero knowledge proof** lets the person demonstrate that approval without sharing the credential or identifying its issuer. The programme and country requested by the verifier remain visible.

## Revocation

Members decide eligibility and can revoke approvals. Wallets refresh the shared revocation state before presenting, and verifiers check that the proof uses an accepted root.

## Future capabilities

The stack is designed to support private payments in the future. Payment distribution, spending, redemption, and settlement are outside Phase 1.
